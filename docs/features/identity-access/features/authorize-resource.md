## User story / outcome
**Actor:** User / ServiceAccount / Agent. **Goal:** Authorize scoped resource access. **Primary owner:** `MOD-IAM`. **Phase:** MVP.

## Pre-conditions
Principal được xác thực; resource tồn tại và có owner rõ ràng.

## Main flow
Resolve verified identity → resolve owned resource → resolve valid grants → evaluate permission+scope+policy context → allow/deny + audit.

## Alternatives / exceptional flow
Grant revoked mid-operation: re-evaluate at execution. Platform role không mặc định được mở dữ liệu tenant.

## Messages and integration contracts
`membership.changed.v1; security.access.denied.v1`. Message names are candidates; verify owner/consumer in [message catalog](../../../contracts/message-catalog.md) before code. Stable envelope, retry and idempotency obey [messaging architecture](../../../architecture/messaging.md).

## Authorization / tenant-scope review
- Principal verified, permission key from [catalog](../../../contracts/permissions.md) and resource owner resolved from trusted store.
- The `organization_id`/scope is validated on read, write, event handler and any background job.
- Audit sensitive decisions with `actorRef`, `resourceRef`, `correlationId`, policy version.

## Negative & recovery scenarios
Role name Admin nhưng không có permission; body tenant bị đổi; expired token/grant; tự cấp quyền cao hơn.
- Generic tests: duplicate request/message, authorization revoked mid-flight, resource from another tenant, crash/restart, stale state/event, partial external failure where applicable.

## Acceptance criteria
Cross-tenant access luôn bị chặn; permission mới được thêm qua data-backed catalog không sửa if-role.
- No hard-coded role name; can add role templates without rewriting handler authorization.
- Linked tests and source commit evidenced before marking `implemented` / `verified`.

## Proposed technical tasks (not implementation evidence)
- Define command/query/API shape; validate domain invariants in owner module.
- Implement contract integration + tenant-safe query; write unit, contract, integration and failure tests.
- Add tracing/audit and update technical-debt / runbook if unresolved risk.

### Proposed responsibility and trust seams

[MOD-IAM](../module.md) retains **Principal, IdentityLink, Session, RoleDefinition and PolicyDecision**. [MOD-TEN](../../tenant-management/module.md) retains Organization, Membership, Invitation and TenantStatus under [FEAT-TEN-001](../../tenant-management/features/membership-lifecycle.md). Project/environment and target ownership remain with MOD-PRJ and MOD-INV respectively; resolve them through authoritative owner contracts, never dashboard projections or direct cross-module table access. [MOD-PAD](../../platform-admin/module.md) owns platform/support grants and support sessions; [MOD-AUD](../../audit-compliance/module.md) owns decision audit records. Target ConnectionProfile/SecretReference/CredentialBinding remain [MOD-SEC](../../connection-secrets/features/secret-binding.md) concerns; they are not local-account credentials or login authority. Collaboration dependencies do not authorize cyclic project references; agree contract direction/composition before wiring.

FEAT-IAM-001 evaluates authorization. Login, password enrollment and recovery are authentication concerns requiring their **own approved feature contract before code**; this edit adds no authentication endpoints. A future authenticated local session identifies a principal only: it cannot supply trusted caller `tenantId`, create membership or imply owner/platform power.

Proposed in-process responsibilities below are review inputs, **not adopted API, data or wire schemas**:

| Seam | Proposed input/authority and denial rule |
|---|---|
| Principal/session trust | An approved authentication boundary must supply verified principal/session identity and current validity/revocation evidence. Resolve status through its trusted owner; missing, invalid, expired, revoked or unavailable evidence denies protected access. A client assertion or cached login success cannot replace validation. ServiceAccount/Agent trust needs its own reviewed identity contract; local user accounts do not authenticate those actors automatically. |
| Tenant/resource ownership | Resolve active membership/tenant status from MOD-TEN and the requested resource's organization/project/environment/target association from its authoritative owner. Caller scope is only a requested selection, cross-checked against both; wrong tenant, suspended/missing ownership or unavailable/stale authority denies. No inference from labels, local profile or provider credentials. |
| Permission/policy evaluation | Combine trusted subject, current membership/grant facts, the action's existing catalog permission and permitted scope, authoritative resource chain, policy revision, time/expiry and relevant conditions. Require a valid scoped grant; deny overrides permit. For example, `target.read` retains its catalog project/environment scope while checking the particular target association. Role labels, parent scope and read permission do not imply other permissions. Missing policy/grant dependencies or unverifiable freshness deny. |
| Platform/support separation | Platform authority cannot read tenant payload. Tenant support requires a separately validated MOD-PAD delegated grant with exact resource/action scope, TTL, reason and required approval; missing/revoked/expired approval or grant denies. Do not invent an OP-P0 support bypass or default platform-to-tenant inheritance. |
| Revocation and disclosure | Revalidate session, membership/grant, ownership and policy before protected use and result disclosure, including delayed work. A permission change invalidates reliance on an earlier permit; re-evaluate at the execution boundary. Restart/reconnect cannot restore old authority by default. Exact freshness/concurrency guarantees require review; do not claim instantaneous cancellation of work already performed. |
| Decision/audit boundary | Return only disclosure-authorized decision metadata; correlate actor/resource, policy revision, reason and outcome with MOD-AUD. No passwords, session credentials or tenant existence leaks in errors/logs/audit. Unavailable mandatory authorization evidence denies; if required audit cannot be recorded, do not proceed with the protected action. Exact durable audit/outage behavior remains a decision. |

### I2.3 — Validation, result and disclosure boundary

The proposed evaluator denies unless **all** required evidence is resolved, well formed, owner-trusted, current and coherent for this invocation. Validate independently, without changing the selection to make a match:

1. Match invocation principal = session subject = membership principal = grant subject. Match requested tenant = target tenant = project tenant = membership tenant = grant tenant, plus environment tenant when present. Match requested target = INV target = PRJ target binding when present. Match INV project = PRJ project, and any INV environment = PRJ environment whose project/tenant match that chain. No foreign-scope association may be accepted by matching a display label.
2. Match requested/granted scope kind and ID exactly. Project scope ID equals the resolved project; environment scope ID equals the resolved environment. The project chain is required in both cases. For an environment-associated target, validate the binding even for project scope. Platform authority never substitutes for tenant evidence; unsupported support/approval paths deny, including expired approval.
3. At each protected boundary use trusted time and the approved skew/clock policy. Require `notBefore <= now < expiresAt` for each applicable interval and `now < deadline`; equality at expiry denies. Enclosing evidence cannot outlive any underlying session/membership/grant/policy/consistency validity. Missing time, unbounded or inverted required intervals, clock rollback/uncertainty outside policy, revocation, inactive/suspended state or freshness uncertainty deny. No arbitrary TTL/skew allowance is selected here.
4. Require current policy/owner revisions and complete condition evaluation under I2.4. Explicit deny, scope mismatch or failed required condition overrides any positive grant. No failure, exception, unknown response, timeout or audit/authority outage may default to permit. A structurally valid record cannot supply missing provenance or consistency.

### I2.4 — Freshness, concurrent revocation and protected-use prerequisites

Proposed protected boundaries are **before protected use** (including provider dispatch or protected cache access) and **before any disclosure** (including delayed results, cached data, exports or a later page/chunk). The trusted enforcement consumer owns these boundaries; IAM owns evaluation, each authority owner owns its facts, and AUD owns audit durability. Internal ownership resolution must itself have a reviewed trusted access boundary and disclose no metadata to the client.

Sequentially reading each owner, then re-reading revisions just before sending data, still leaves races during reads and between the final check and use. A revision vector is diagnostic binding, not an atomic snapshot, a revocation fence or proof that all facts were simultaneously valid. A fresh timestamp or a successful audit append does not close that gap either. **No runtime permit is justified until an approved consistency protocol defines a coherent decision/use boundary across the participating owners and enforcer.** This document invents no cross-module transaction and promises no instantaneous revocation.

Before adoption the assigned owners/security reviewer must define: which changes invalidate authority; the decision/use and disclosure ordering points; how conflicting owner versions, target reassignment and concurrent revoke are detected or excluded; the lifetime and enforceability of any consistency evidence; clock/partition/time-out behavior; and what happens when revoke races with an already-started read or a partially disclosed response. The protocol must explicitly distinguish revoke effective before the protected boundary (deny), revoke after an already completed boundary (cannot undo prior disclosure), and ambiguous ordering (withhold further use/disclosure). Any permitted in-flight window requires explicit human acceptance and a bounded guarantee; it cannot be inferred from a cache TTL. A “final recheck” without enforceable ordering is insufficient.

When a revision/status changes, cancellation, expiry or uncertainty occurs, discard eligibility and re-resolve required authority for any new attempt; no permissive stale fallback. Discard unauthorized delayed results and do not publish them into caches accessible under another subject/selection. Reconnect/restart, duplicate requests and stale invalidation events cannot resurrect authority. Caches may optimize resolution only within the reviewed protocol and must still revalidate protected boundaries; cached decisions are never sufficient. Current checks cannot retract bytes already released, so streaming/partial disclosure is excluded from the first adoption scope. Crash/unknown execution or audit outcomes require reconciliation, not automatic replay of protected work. Later side-effecting workflows need their own idempotency, inbox/outbox, retry and unknown-outcome contracts; this in-process proposal adds no message semantics.

#### I2.7d — Protected-use and disclosure ordering proposal

Use and disclosure are separate logical commitments, not new runtime states or APIs. For a synthetic acceptance setup, **U** is admission of the exact protected read/cache access; **D** is irreversible handoff of the exact bounded result to the approved caller-delivery boundary. A network acknowledgement or successful client receipt is not D. IAM eligibility, an audit append and completion of data retrieval are each distinct from U/D. The actual implementation boundary corresponding to D, its payload budget and transport behavior require explicit maintainer/security acceptance before runtime adoption.

Recommend these obligations for a future consistency protocol:

1. All changes that can invalidate the decision participate: principal/session disable/revoke, tenant suspension, membership/assignment removal/expiry, RoleDefinition/catalog/policy change or explicit deny, project/environment lifecycle or classification/policy change, target disable/delete/reassignment and binding change. Scope/selection change, cancellation and deadline expiry independently invalidate the invocation. Time expiry must be enforced at the boundary, not only by a scheduled invalidation message.
2. Establish coherent current owner facts and a defined ordering between each relevant effective invalidation and **each** protected commitment. Define when an invalidation becomes effective and what its acknowledgement promises. A requested revoke with unknown commit outcome is unresolved, not a successful revocation or evidence of continued authority. Do not report revocation complete while the approved ordering guarantee is unproved. An older in-flight writer/result must not reinstate authority after a completed revoke; recovery needs an approved durable rule before runtime adoption.
3. If an invalidation is effective before U, perform no protected read/cache access. If U precedes invalidation but invalidation precedes D, discard the delayed protected result and release none of it. If D is ordered first, already committed disclosure cannot be recalled; no subsequent protected use or disclosure may rely on the old decision. Concurrent/partitioned operations whose order cannot be established withhold further access. Equal timestamps do not choose a winner.
4. Admission at U cannot reserve a future right to D. Resolve/evaluate current authority again for D and bind the result to the exact subject/session, tenant, target, scope, selection and the authority revisions under which it was obtained. For this first candidate, an intervening authority/binding change discards that result rather than relabeling it under new evidence. Any new attempt starts with current owner resolution; retries are not continuation of an old permit.
5. Required durable audit must be satisfied for the exact stage **without opening a check-to-use gap while waiting**. The eventual protocol must keep the ordered conditions effective through the commitment or reject/revalidate them there. Holding an admission exclusion across audit latency, or conditionally admitting only if all authority still qualifies, has different cost/recovery trade-offs; neither mechanism is selected. A last sequential recheck after audit still fails this obligation. Revalidation that creates a new decision needs matching audit evidence.
6. Assemble and validate a bounded result privately before D. No streaming, pagination continuation, partial-result fallback, shared cache publication or background notification can disclose data early. Later page/export/retry is a new invocation requiring current checks. A changed selection cannot receive the old result; protected caches require the same authorization boundaries as a read. No persistent authorization or data-cache implementation is selected.

The recommended revocation guarantee is **no newly admitted U/D after an invalidation effective under the approved ordering protocol**, with no intentional stale-authority grace period. This is a proposed contract obligation, not a claim of instantaneous distributed revocation. Bytes handed off at D may remain in transport or arrive after revoke; a buffered response also can fail partway through delivery. Excluding application streaming does not make network delivery atomic. The permitted commitment point, residual transport disclosure and cancellation/recovery behavior require exact human decision. If that residual is unacceptable, keep disclosure denied until a separately reviewed design can meet the chosen guarantee; do not silently redefine “revoked” or claim retractable delivery.

**Protocol adoption proof required:** identify all invalidating writers and the enforcing consumer; show which mechanism orders their effective changes with U/D, which facts/versions it covers, how expired/abandoned admissions and process restarts are handled, and why delayed writers cannot bypass revocation. Demonstrate both orderings and uncertainty/crash interleavings. A local lock covering only IAM, timestamps, sequential owner reads, a cache TTL, a revision vector or an audit receipt alone fails this proof. These are obligations for the next explicit decision, not a selected infrastructure/persistence design. Without that decision, continued total inventory denial is the recommended operational outcome.
